## Overview

Papa Bois - also called Papa Bwa, Maitre Bois ('master of the woods') or Daddy Bouchon ('hairy man') - is one of the central figures of Trinidad and Tobago folklore, with closely related traditions in Saint Lucia, Dominica, and Grenada. He is described as an aged but powerfully built man covered in hair, with cloven hooves (most often the left foot) and small horns, and a beard that may be made of leaves. He carries a hollowed-out cow horn that he sounds to warn the animals of an approaching hunter. Papa Bois is the protector of the forest and its creatures: respectful travellers and ethical hunters are granted safe passage, but those who hunt wantonly, kill nursing animals, or destroy the forest are punished - sometimes by being lured deep into the bush in the form of a deer, sometimes by being made to marry his consort, the river-spirit Mama D'Lo. The figure synthesizes West African, French, and Indigenous Amerindian elements characteristic of Trinidad's Creole folklore.

Folklorists treat Papa Bois as a Creole syncretic figure that emerged from West African, French, and Amerindian sources, functioning as a moral and ecological teaching figure rather than an actual entity.
